---
anchor: GEN
title: Joseph as a type of Christ
tags: typology, joseph, allegory
---

Joseph's life prefigures Christ: the beloved son sold for silver, who forgave his brothers, became the savior of the world by providing the bread of life, and brought his Gentile bride and children to his Father, ultimately revealing the cross to be the instrument of blessing.

* The beloved son of the father, envied by his brothers ([37:3–4](ref:GEN 37:3-4), [37:11](ref:GEN 37:11)).
* His brothers plotted to kill him and instead sold him for twenty pieces of silver ([37:28](ref:GEN 37:28)); *cf.* Christ betrayed for thirty ([Matthew 26:15](ref:MAT 26:15)).
* Cast into a pit (a "cistern," λάκκος / *lakkos*) and left for dead ([37:24](ref:GEN 37:24)), yet raised out of it by God's providence.
* Falsely accused and unjustly condemned, he descended into prison ([39:20](ref:GEN 39:20)) as Christ descended among the condemned.
* In prison he interpreted two dreams, both fulfilled "in three days" ([40:12](ref:GEN 40:12), [40:18](ref:GEN 40:18)), pointing to the Resurrection and Eucharist:
   * the baker's dream of bread ending in a body hung on a tree ([40:19](ref:GEN 40:19); *cf.* [Acts 5:30](ref:ACT 5:30), [Galatians 3:13](ref:GAL 3:13)), and
   * the cupbearer's dream of the vine and its wine ending in restoration and life ([40:9–13](ref:GEN 40:9-13); *cf.* [John 15:1](ref:JHN 15:1)).
* Raised from prison to sit at Pharaoh's right hand, second only to the throne ([41:40–41](ref:GEN 41:40-41)); *cf.* Christ exalted to the Father's right hand ([Acts 2:33](ref:ACT 2:33)).
* He became the savior of the world by storing and distributing grain, the bread of life, so that "all countries came to Egypt to buy" from him ([41:57](ref:GEN 41:57); *cf.* [John 6:35](ref:JHN 6:35)).
* He married a Gentile bride, Asenath, and had two sons by her ([41:45](ref:GEN 41:45), [41:50–52](ref:GEN 41:50-52)), just as Christ weds the Church drawn from the nations ([Ephesians 5:31–32](ref:EPH 5:31-32)).
* He forgave his brothers who had betrayed him, declaring that what they intended for evil, God intended for good, to save many people alive ([50:20](ref:GEN 50:20); *cf.* [Luke 23:34](ref:LUK 23:34)).
* He brought his Gentile-born sons to his father, who adopted them as his own ([48:5](ref:GEN 48:5)).
* The younger son, Ephraim, was set above the elder, Manasseh, and given the greater blessing: Jacob deliberately crossing his hands over Joseph's protest ([48:17–20](ref:GEN 48:17-20)); so too the Gentiles, latecomers to the covenant, were granted precedence over Israel, the elder (*cf.* [Romans 9:12](ref:ROM 9:12); [11:11-12](ref:ROM 11:11-12); [Matthew 21:43](ref:MAT 21:43)).
* In blessing them, Jacob crossed his hands (ἐναλλάξ / *enallax*, "crosswise," [48:14](ref:GEN 48:14)), setting his right hand on the younger, thus revealing the cross as the instrument by which the father blesses his children (*cf.* [Hebrews 11:21](ref:HEB 11:21)).
