title: Service through surrender
tags: diaconate
---

>Contemporary scriptural commentators have seen the conjunction "and" (καὶ [*kai*]) between the two key words διακονῆσαι [(*diakonēsai*)] and δοῦναι [(*dounai*)] in Mark 10:45 as explanatory or exegetical. Jesus serves by surrendering; his giving entails offering his life. Indeed, while the word for "service at altar" (λατρεύειν [*latreuein*], or worship) is employed in the New Testament to describe the relationship of Jesus as Son of God to his Father (cf. [Matt 4:10](ref:MAT 4:10)), the term for "service at table" (dιακονία [*diakonia*]) is generally used to describe the relationship of Jesus as the Son of Man to the world. Thus, *diakonia* came to be accepted as a vehicle for expressing the most profound concern of Christians for others as well as the most dignified identification of Christians with Christ himself. *Diakonia* assumed the sense of fellowship and deification alike, embracing the concepts of communication with the world and communion with God.[^fn1]

[^fn1]: Chryssavgis, John. *Remembering and Reclaiming Diakonia: The Diaconate Yesterday and Today*. Brookline, MA: Holy Cross Orthodox Press, 2009, 23.
