Vv. 6–10 likely contain some or all of an early Christian hymn. Elsewhere [TCENT](/about/texts-and-translations/#tcent) lays out hymnography poetically, but for some reason did not do so here. Here is a proposed layout:

>Christ Jesus...<br />
>who, though he was in the form of God,<br />
>did not consider equality with God<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;as something to be exploited,<br />
>but emptied himself<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;by taking the form of a servant<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and being born in the likeness of men.<br />
>And being found in appearance as a man,<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;he humbled himself<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;by becoming obedient to the point of death,<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;even death on a cross.<br />
>Therefore God highly exalted him<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and gave him the name that is above every other name,<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;so that at the name of Jesus<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;every knee should bow,<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;in heaven and on earth and under the earth,<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and every tongue confess<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;that Jesus Christ is Lord,<br />
>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;to the glory of God the Father.

Fr. Pat Reardon suspects that St. Paul is here translating an originally Aramaic hymn into Greek.[^r1]

[^r1]: Patrick Henry Reardon, *Reclaiming the Atonement: An Orthodox Theology of Redemption: The Incarnate Word*, vol. 1 (Chesterton, IN: Ancient Faith Publishing, 2015), 98.
