title: Parable of the Fall and redemption
tags: typology
---

The Church Fathers see in the Parable of the Good Samaritan a parable of humanity's Fall and redemption in three movements.[^r]

**1. The Fall.** The man goes "down to Jericho from Jerusalem," departing God's presence by his own choice. The robbers leave him *half dead*: fallen, yet not wholly depraved, but unable to save himself. The priest and Levite who pass by represent the Law, which cannot justify ("For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins" [[Heb. 10:4](ref:HEB 10:4)]).

**2. The Redemption.** The Samaritan is the Son who "emptied Himself" ([Php 2:7](ref:PHP 2:7)), becoming poor that we might become rich ([2 Cor 8:9](ref:2CO 8:9)). He *sees* the man, heals him through the Sacraments (baptismal water, holy Chrism, eucharistic wine), brings him to the inn (the Church), and cares for him before ascending as the Great High Priest.

**3. The Return.** He tells the innkeeper, "*When* I return"&mdash;not *if*. His return is certain; He will appear "the second time, apart from sin, unto salvation."[^r2] The parable ends in the inn where His friends abide in hope.  "It is imperative for their souls' health that they abide within this inn, to which our Samaritan has sworn to return."[^r3] Only those who await His return know the meaning of history.

[^r]: Adapted from Patrick Henry Reardon, *Reclaiming the Atonement: An Orthodox Theology of Redemption: The Incarnate Word*, vol. 1 (Chesterton, IN: Ancient Faith Publishing, 2015), 81–84.

[^r2]: *Ibid.,* 83.

[^r3]: *Ibid.,* 84.
